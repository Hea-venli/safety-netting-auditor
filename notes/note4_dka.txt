Patient 4 was admitted on 19/06/2026 with a 3-day history of vomiting, abdominal pain, polyuria and thirst on a background of viral illness, with reduced insulin intake due to poor appetite. Diagnosis: diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) precipitated by intercurrent viral illness, in known Type 1 diabetes (diagnosed age 11, basal-bolus insulin).
Management: Fixed-rate IV insulin infusion per JBDS protocol; IV fluids with potassium replacement; hourly ketone/glucose monitoring; 10 percent glucose added once CBG below 14; VTE prophylaxis. DKA resolved at approximately 20 hours; converted back to usual subcutaneous basal-bolus regimen with overlap. Reviewed by Diabetes Specialist Nurse - sick-day rules reinforced.
Discharge medications: Insulin glargine 18 units nocte (unchanged); insulin aspart per mealtime regimen (unchanged).
Follow-up: DSN telephone review within 1 week; diabetes clinic in 6 weeks; GP letter for information.
Safety-netting: Never stop insulin, even when unwell or not eating - this is the most important message. Follow sick-day rules: check glucose and ketones every 2-4 hours when ill, keep hydrated, take correction doses as advised. Seek help if ketones above 3 mmol/L, persistent vomiting, glucose staying high despite corrections, or drowsiness/confusion. NHS 111 if unwell but stable; call 999 / A&E if vomiting repeatedly, breathing fast/deeply, abdominal pain, or drowsiness - these suggest DKA is returning.
